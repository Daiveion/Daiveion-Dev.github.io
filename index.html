CEAS ’30 · ACCEND BS/MS Marian Spencer Scholar UHP

I learned to run my own schedule before anyone asked me to. Everything since has been built on that.

I’m a first-year computer engineering student at the University of Cincinnati, in the ACCEND accelerated BS/MS track and a Marian Spencer Scholar. I finished high school second in my class at an online academy, which meant no bells and no one checking whether I’d started. I had to learn to hold myself to a plan, ask for help before I was drowning, and keep going through stretches that had nothing academic about them.

That habit built the rest: a full scholarship, a software engineering internship at Procter & Gamble before my first college lecture, and a provisional patent on EagleVision, a digital microscope platform for schools that can’t fund a lab bench per student. My faith is the reason I keep aiming work outward — I’d rather build something that gives a person access they didn’t have than something that only looks impressive.

At UC I want to explore where hardware meets software: embedded systems, computer architecture, and machine learning from the inside, not just the application layer. Through the UHP I want to find the people doing work I don’t know how to do yet.

My goals: finish ACCEND debt-free, take EagleVision from patent to a real classroom pilot, and graduate with a record of what I learned — not just what I won.

Projects

The work behind the reflection. Each of these taught me something I couldn’t have read about.

EagleVision — digital microscope platform

An EdTech platform that turns a digital microscope into something a whole classroom can see, use, and record from — built for schools that can’t fund a lab bench per student. A provisional patent has been filed, and I’ve presented it to an elementary school, which was the first time I watched kids use something I’d made.

Next: move from prototype and patent to a classroom pilot with real teachers and real constraints.

Procter & Gamble — Software Engineering Intern

Consumer Digital Products. I built an internal tool for compliant consumer data use — my first experience writing software where the hard part wasn’t the code, it was understanding the rules the code had to respect and the people who’d depend on it after I left.

ScamShield AI — INTERalliance competition entry

An AI classifier concept for catching financial scam messages before they land, designed around a specific person we wanted to protect rather than an abstract user. Mid-project the submission format changed, and we rebuilt the deliverable from a full presentation into a 30-second animated piece with voiceover. Learning to pivot the format without losing the idea was the real takeaway.
